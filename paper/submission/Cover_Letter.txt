1. Operational Context and Practical Motivation:
Although conventional literature demonstrates that software AES implementations achieve acceptable throughput via precomputed lookup tables, their substantial 4,096-byte memory footprint frequently induces acute L1 data-cache eviction and exposes timing side channels on microcontrollers lacking AES-NI. In modern cyber-physical systems, automotive CAN-FD in-vehicle communication, and constrained telemetry networks (such as CoAP and MQTT-SN), edge microcontrollers lack dedicated hardware cryptographic accelerators (e.g., AES-NI) and possess restricted on-chip cache memory. Conversely, pure ARX ciphers (e.g., SIMON and SPECK) lack closed-form wide-trail security arguments and require high round counts (32 to 68 rounds) that penalize short-packet transmission latency.

2. Architectural Contribution and Mathematical Formulation:
To resolve this fundamental operational trade-off, this manuscript introduces Tesseract-128, demonstrating that coupling discrete double-isoclinic rotations from the Lie group SO(4) with 32-bit Add-Rotate-XOR modular diffusion disperses byte coordinates across orthogonal planes without invariant subspaces, requiring only 192 bytes of static lookup tables. By mapping the 16-byte cipher state onto the vertices of a 4D hypercube {0,1}^4, the linear layer achieves algebraic transitivity and optimal diffusion while fitting completely within a single 256-byte cache line or micro-SRAM scratchpad, completely eliminating table-induced cache-collision attacks.

3. Automated Cryptanalysis and Empirical Performance:
Within a word-level MILP model of the ARX carry chain, automated searches establish lower bounds of 78 active S-boxes against differential cryptanalysis (P_diff <= 2^-468 < 2^-128) and 108 active S-boxes against linear cryptanalysis across 8 rounds; these are model-relative bounds rather than proofs of security, and they are consistent with our empirical hardware benchmarks achieving a 2.88x encryption latency reduction over software AES on 64-byte automotive CAN-FD telemetry frames.
Key rigorous verification findings established in this work include:
- Tool-Aided Security Margins: Within a word-level truncated-differential MILP model, a lower bound of >= 78 active S-boxes is obtained against differential cryptanalysis (P_diff <= 2^-468 < 2^-128) and >= 108 against linear cryptanalysis over 8 rounds; this is a model-relative bound, not a proof of immunity.
- Miss-in-the-Middle Termination: Automated searches establish zero impossible differential and zero-correlation trails at >= 5 rounds, securing a comfortable 4-round safety margin.
- Multiset Integral Balance: Simulations across 1,280 Lambda-sets (16 active positions x 20 keys) confirm multiset balance termination by Round 4.
- Statistical Randomness: Generated keystreams pass all 15 statistical tests in NIST SP 800-22 Rev. 1a and GM/T 0005-2021 without exception.
- Hardware Acceleration: SIMD vectorization (AVX2/SSSE3) achieves single-cycle permutation execution via pshufb, delivering 254.18 MB/s in CTR mode and 208.80 MB/s in authenticated Tesseract-GCM mode.
- Real-World Telemetry Impact: In an automotive CAN-FD testbed, Tesseract-GCM processes 64-byte frames in 1,267 clock cycles (~0.37 us at 3.4 GHz), representing a 2.88x speedup over software AES and adding under 0.3% transmission overhead to the physical bus.
